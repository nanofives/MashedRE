Observations (is 9176/9178 enough?):
- Most Ghidra-start losses were arity errors from Ghidra's callee prototypes (0x004b3f90, 0x005c9d00 stub). Once fixed, deferred `add esp` flushes matched exactly: no evidence of a cleanup-merging difference.
- `in_EAX` (0x00498d20, and the 0x004570a0 call inside 0x00457100) is plain same-TU static-function register passing, reproduced by 9176 without /GL.
- Inlining: 9176 /O2 inlines the 81-byte single-caller static 0x004570a0; the original kept it out of line. `/O2 /Ob1` reproduces the original and leaves the other five functions at their scores. Either the game used /Ob1, the source blocked inlining, or 9466's inliner heuristics differ. This is the most specific lead for a 9466 test.
- FP: named globals vs literals change operand order (9176 loads a named global first for mem*global in either source order, a literal second). The original's 0x005ce4e4..0x005ce4f8 cluster is used only by 0x00457100, which fits literals in that TU. But with literals 9176 folds `u*200*1.25` to one multiply, rewrites `z*-0.1f+y` as `y-z*0.1f`, and factors `v*C+fx+C`. The original does none of these. Parentheses block them in 9176, so this is not proof of a 9466 difference.
- No difference seen that only a compiler-version change could explain. The leftovers are scheduling/operand-order choices (2 functions, about 12 instructions in total) that the source spellings I tried did not move.
